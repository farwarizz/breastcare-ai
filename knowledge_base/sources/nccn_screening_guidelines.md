# NCCN Breast Cancer Screening Guidelines

## Overview

The National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) provides evidence-based guidelines for breast cancer screening and risk assessment. These recommendations stratify patients based on age, family history, genetic risk, and prior breast conditions.

## Average-Risk Screening Recommendations

Women at average risk (no significant family history, no known genetic mutations, no prior chest radiation) should follow age-based screening:

**Age 25–39**: Clinical breast examination every 1–3 years. Mammography not routinely recommended. Risk assessment and counseling about breast awareness.

**Age 40–44**: Annual mammography is optional. Shared decision-making between patient and provider. If screening is initiated, annual digital mammography or digital breast tomosynthesis (DBT) is recommended.

**Age 45–54**: Annual mammography recommended. Digital breast tomosynthesis (3D mammography) preferred when available. Clinical breast examination every year.

**Age 55–74**: Annual or biennial mammography (patient choice). Transition to biennial screening is acceptable. Continue as long as life expectancy exceeds 10 years.

**Age 75+**: Continue screening if good health and life expectancy ≥ 10 years. Shared decision-making regarding continuation.

## High-Risk Screening Recommendations

Women at high risk (≥20% lifetime risk by validated models) should receive enhanced screening:

Annual mammography starting at age 30 (but not before age 25). Annual breast MRI starting at age 25–29 or at time of earliest known risk. Consider alternating mammography and MRI every 6 months for maximum detection sensitivity.

## Genetic Risk Factors

BRCA1 mutation carriers have a 55–72% lifetime risk of breast cancer. BRCA2 mutation carriers have a 45–69% lifetime risk. Both should begin annual MRI at age 25 and annual mammography at age 30, or individualized based on family history of earliest onset.

Other high-penetrance genes include TP53 (Li-Fraumeni), PTEN (Cowden), CDH1, PALB2, and ATM. Each requires individualized screening protocols.

## Family History Criteria for Enhanced Screening

First-degree relative (mother, sister, daughter) diagnosed with breast cancer before age 50. Two or more first-degree relatives with breast cancer at any age. Male relative with breast cancer. Known genetic mutation in the family. Prior chest radiation between ages 10–30.

## Risk Assessment Tools

Gail Model: Estimates 5-year and lifetime risk using age, race, age at menarche, age at first live birth, number of first-degree relatives, and prior biopsies. Validated for women ≥35 years. Limitation: does not account for second-degree relatives or paternal family history.

Tyrer-Cuzick (IBIS) Model: More comprehensive — includes both maternal and paternal family history, BRCA status, breast density, BMI, HRT use, and reproductive history. Generally considered more accurate for high-risk assessment.

BRCAPRO: Bayesian model estimating BRCA1/BRCA2 carrier probability using family history of breast and ovarian cancer.
