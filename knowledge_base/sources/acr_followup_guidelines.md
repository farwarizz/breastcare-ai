# BI-RADS Assessment Categories 3, 4, and 5 — Follow-Up and Biopsy

The final BI-RADS assessment category sets how a finding is managed. The likelihoods of malignancy below are the ranges the American College of Radiology attaches to each category.

## BI-RADS Category 3: Probably Benign

Category 3 means a likelihood of malignancy of 2% or less. It is used only after a complete diagnostic work-up, never at screening. Typical examples are a solitary non-calcified circumscribed solid mass, a focal asymmetry with no correlate on ultrasound, or a solitary group of round calcifications.

Category 3 findings are managed by surveillance rather than biopsy: usually a follow-up mammogram at 6 months, then bilateral mammograms at about 12 and 24 months. After about 2 to 3 years of stability, the finding is reassessed as benign (category 2), or earlier if it resolves. A biopsy may still be chosen after shared decision-making with the patient, for a valid clinical reason, or when follow-up is impractical. If the finding grows or changes suspiciously, it is upgraded and biopsied.

## BI-RADS Category 4: Suspicious

Category 4 covers a likelihood of malignancy above 2% and below 95%. The recommended management is a tissue diagnosis, usually an image-guided core needle biopsy. The category is subdivided by level of suspicion.

### Category 4A

Category 4A means low suspicion for malignancy: above 2% and up to 10%. A biopsy is recommended, but a benign result is expected. After a benign result that is concordant with the imaging, the radiologist sets the follow-up.

### Category 4B

Category 4B means moderate suspicion for malignancy: above 10% and up to 50%. A benign biopsy result needs careful correlation with the imaging to decide whether it is concordant.

### Category 4C

Category 4C means high suspicion for malignancy: above 50% and below 95%. A malignant result is expected, so a benign result is usually treated as discordant unless it specifically explains the imaging finding.

## BI-RADS Category 5: Highly Suggestive of Malignancy

Category 5 means a likelihood of malignancy of 95% or more, and a tissue diagnosis is required. A non-malignant result from a needle biopsy is typically considered discordant. Classic examples are an irregular, spiculated, high-density mass, or new fine linear-branching calcifications in a segmental distribution.

## Concordance Review

Radiologic–pathologic concordance means that the biopsy result satisfactorily explains what was seen on imaging. For example, a fibroadenoma explains a well-defined oval mass. The radiologist who performed the biopsy determines concordance, documents it and communicates it. A discordant result needs a repeat image-guided biopsy or surgical excision. Follow-up after a benign concordant biopsy is individualised.

## What Happens After an Abnormal Mammogram?

1. Additional diagnostic mammographic views or a breast ultrasound may be recommended to characterise the finding further.
2. If a biopsy is recommended (category 4 or 5), an image-guided core needle biopsy is the usual first procedure.
3. The pathology result is compared with the imaging to check that they agree (concordance).
4. If cancer is confirmed, staging and treatment planning begin with the care team.
5. If the biopsy shows a benign result that agrees with the imaging, follow-up imaging is scheduled as the radiologist advises.

An AI risk estimate does not replace this clinical workflow. A risk estimate of any level does not bypass the need for clinical assessment, imaging interpretation by a radiologist and, where indicated, biopsy.
