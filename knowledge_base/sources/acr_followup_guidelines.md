# ACR Follow-Up Guidelines — BI-RADS Categories 3, 4, and 5

## BI-RADS Category 3: Probably Benign

A BI-RADS Category 3 assessment indicates a finding with a less than 2% likelihood of malignancy. The recommended management is short-interval follow-up mammography at 6 months, followed by bilateral mammography at 12 months and 24 months. If stability is demonstrated at 24 months, the assessment can be downgraded to Category 2 (benign).

Category 3 findings should not be biopsied as the initial management. Biopsy may be appropriate if the patient has anxiety, if follow-up is not feasible, or if the lesion changes during follow-up.

Common Category 3 findings include: a group of round punctate calcifications, a non-palpable round or oval circumscribed mass on a first mammogram, and focal asymmetry without associated suspicious features.

## BI-RADS Category 4: Suspicious

A BI-RADS Category 4 assessment indicates a finding for which biopsy should be considered. Category 4 encompasses a wide range of malignancy probability (2%–95%) and is subdivided:

### Category 4A — Low suspicion for malignancy (2%–10%)
Biopsy is recommended but benign pathology is expected. If the pathology result is benign and concordant with imaging findings, 6-month follow-up is appropriate.

### Category 4B — Intermediate suspicion for malignancy (10%–50%)
There is genuine concern for malignancy. Benign histology requires close correlation with imaging to determine whether the result is concordant. Discordant results require repeat biopsy or excision.

### Category 4C — Moderate concern for malignancy (50%–95%)
Malignancy is more likely than not. A benign pathology result should be considered discordant unless it specifically explains the imaging finding. Re-biopsy or excision is typically required for a benign discordant result.

## BI-RADS Category 5: Highly Suggestive of Malignancy

A BI-RADS Category 5 assessment indicates a finding that is highly suggestive of malignancy, with a greater than 95% probability of malignancy. Biopsy is required. A benign pathology result should be considered discordant; excision or re-biopsy is required.

Category 5 findings include: spiculated high-density masses, pleomorphic calcifications in a linear or branching distribution, and a combination of highly suspicious features.

## Concordance Review

Radiological–pathological concordance is the process of comparing the imaging findings with the biopsy pathology result. A benign pathology result is concordant if it specifically explains the imaging abnormality (e.g., a fibroadenoma explaining a well-defined oval mass). A benign pathology result is discordant if it does not explain the imaging finding. Discordant results require multidisciplinary review and typically lead to re-biopsy or surgical excision.

## What Happens After an Abnormal Mammogram?

1. Additional diagnostic mammography views or breast ultrasound may be recommended to further characterise the finding.
2. If a biopsy is recommended (Category 4 or 5), image-guided core needle biopsy is the standard procedure.
3. Biopsy results are reviewed in a multidisciplinary meeting including radiology, pathology, and surgery.
4. If malignancy is confirmed, staging and treatment planning are initiated.
5. If biopsy shows benign concordant pathology, follow-up imaging is scheduled per the BI-RADS protocol.

An AI risk estimate does not replace this clinical workflow. A risk estimate of any level does not bypass the need for clinical assessment, imaging interpretation by a radiologist, and — where indicated — biopsy.
