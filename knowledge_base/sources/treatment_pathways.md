# Breast Cancer Treatment Pathways

## Overview

Breast cancer treatment is multimodal and individualized. The treatment pathway depends on cancer stage, tumor biology (receptor status, grade), patient factors (age, comorbidities, preferences), and genomic profiling results.

## Treatment Modalities

### Surgery

**Breast-conserving surgery (lumpectomy/partial mastectomy)**: Removes the tumor with a margin of normal tissue. Preferred for early-stage cancers (Stage I-II) when feasible. Typically followed by radiation therapy. Equivalent survival to mastectomy for appropriately selected patients.

**Mastectomy**: Complete removal of breast tissue. Indicated when tumor is large relative to breast size, multicentric disease, prior radiation, or patient preference. Options include simple mastectomy, modified radical mastectomy, and skin-sparing/nipple-sparing mastectomy.

**Sentinel lymph node biopsy**: Identifies the first draining lymph node(s) to determine if cancer has spread. If negative, full axillary dissection is avoided, reducing lymphedema risk.

### Radiation Therapy

**Whole breast radiation**: Standard after lumpectomy. Typically 3–6 weeks of daily treatments. Reduces local recurrence by approximately 50%.

**Partial breast irradiation**: Targets only the tumor bed. Shorter course (1–2 weeks). Appropriate for selected low-risk patients.

**Post-mastectomy radiation**: Recommended for tumors > 5 cm, positive margins, or ≥ 4 positive lymph nodes. Reduces chest wall recurrence.

### Systemic Therapy

**Chemotherapy**: Administered before (neoadjuvant) or after (adjuvant) surgery. Common regimens: AC-T (doxorubicin/cyclophosphamide followed by taxol), TC (docetaxel/cyclophosphamide). Neoadjuvant chemotherapy can shrink tumors to enable breast-conserving surgery.

**Hormonal (endocrine) therapy**: For ER-positive and/or PR-positive cancers (approximately 70% of breast cancers). Tamoxifen for premenopausal women. Aromatase inhibitors (anastrozole, letrozole, exemestane) for postmenopausal women. Duration: typically 5–10 years.

**Targeted therapy**: HER2-positive cancers (approximately 20% of breast cancers) treated with trastuzumab (Herceptin), pertuzumab, or T-DM1 (ado-trastuzumab emtansine). CDK4/6 inhibitors (palbociclib, ribociclib) for ER-positive/HER2-negative metastatic disease. PARP inhibitors (olaparib, talazoparib) for BRCA-mutated cancers.

**Immunotherapy**: Pembrolizumab (Keytruda) for triple-negative breast cancer with PD-L1 expression. Typically combined with chemotherapy. Emerging role in neoadjuvant setting.

## Treatment by Stage

**Stage 0 (DCIS)**: Lumpectomy with radiation, or mastectomy. Hormonal therapy if ER-positive. No chemotherapy needed.

**Stage I**: Lumpectomy with radiation (preferred). Sentinel node biopsy. Adjuvant systemic therapy based on tumor biology and genomic testing (e.g., Oncotype DX).

**Stage II**: Lumpectomy or mastectomy depending on tumor size and breast size ratio. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy may be considered to enable breast conservation. Axillary staging required.

**Stage III (locally advanced)**: Neoadjuvant chemotherapy standard to downstage tumor. Surgery followed by radiation. Full systemic therapy.

**Stage IV (metastatic)**: Treatment goal is disease control and quality of life, not cure. Systemic therapy (endocrine, chemotherapy, targeted) based on tumor biology. Palliative radiation for symptomatic metastases. Surgery for select cases only.

## Genomic Testing

**Oncotype DX**: 21-gene recurrence score for ER-positive, HER2-negative, node-negative/limited disease. Score < 16: endocrine therapy alone. Score 16–25: age-dependent decision. Score ≥ 26: chemotherapy benefit likely.

**MammaPrint**: 70-gene signature classifying tumors as low or high genomic risk. Guides chemotherapy decisions in early-stage disease.
