# BI-RADS Mass Descriptors — Margin, Shape, and Density

BI-RADS is the American College of Radiology's lexicon for describing breast imaging findings. The inputs in this app use the terms of the 4th edition, which the UCI Mammographic Mass data were coded with. The 5th edition (2013) and the BI-RADS v2025 update renamed or removed some of them, as noted under each term. Percentages quoted below come from specific published series and vary between studies; they are context, not a risk estimate for an individual.

## Mass Margin

Margin is the border of a mass on the mammogram. Circumscribed margins are reassuring, while indistinct (ill-defined) and spiculated margins are suspicious.

### Circumscribed Margin

A circumscribed margin is well defined: at least 75% of the border is sharply demarcated, with an abrupt transition between the mass and the surrounding tissue. Circumscribed masses are usually benign; examples include fibroadenomas and cysts. In a large study of non-palpable, non-calcified, circumscribed solid masses followed with mammography, 19 of 1,403 (1.4%) proved malignant. That figure applies to that specific group, not to every circumscribed mass.

### Microlobulated Margin

A microlobulated margin has short-cycle undulations along the border of the mass. The 4th and 5th editions listed it as a suspicious descriptor. The BI-RADS v2025 update removed it to avoid confusion with the shape term "lobulated"; such a margin is now described as indistinct. Published probabilities of malignancy for this margin vary widely between series, so no single figure is given here.

### Obscured Margin

An obscured margin is one where 25% or more of the border is hidden by overlapping normal fibroglandular tissue, while the visible part is circumscribed. Because the margin cannot be fully assessed, the mass needs further diagnostic work-up, such as additional mammographic views or ultrasound, before a final assessment.

### Ill-defined Margin

An ill-defined margin, called "indistinct" since the 5th edition, has no clear demarcation between all or part of the mass and the surrounding tissue. It is a suspicious finding. In one biopsy series, 29 of 66 masses (44%) with this margin were malignant.

### Spiculated Margin

A spiculated margin has thin lines radiating from the edge of the mass. It is one of the most suspicious features on a mammogram: in a large series of biopsied lesions, 81% of spiculated masses were malignant. A spiculated mass warrants a tissue diagnosis, although a minority turn out to be benign.

## Mass Shape

Shape describes the outline of the mass.

### Round Shape

A round mass is spherical, ball-shaped or circular. Round masses are most often benign, such as cysts and fibroadenomas, particularly when the margin is circumscribed.

### Oval Shape

An oval mass is elliptical or egg-shaped, with one axis longer than the others. Oval shape, especially with a circumscribed margin, is generally reassuring.

### Lobular Shape

"Lobular" is the 4th-edition term, used in the UCI data, for a mass with an undulating contour. The 5th edition removed it, and the BI-RADS v2025 update brought it back as "lobulated": a mass with one or more indentations that give it an undulating contour. Lobulated masses are usually benign but are considered slightly more suspicious than oval ones.

### Irregular Shape

An irregular mass is neither round, oval nor lobulated. Irregular shape is a suspicious feature: in one series of biopsied lesions, 73% of irregular masses were malignant. An irregular, spiculated, high-density mass is a classic appearance of a BI-RADS category 5 finding.

## Mass Density

Density compares how much the mass attenuates X-rays with an equal volume of fibroglandular tissue.

### High Density

A high-density mass attenuates more X-rays than the same volume of fibroglandular tissue. High density is associated with malignancy: in one biopsy series, 70.2% of high-density masses were malignant, compared with 22.3% of iso-dense or low-density masses.

### Iso-density

An iso-dense mass, described as "equal density" in current terminology, has the same attenuation as fibroglandular tissue. On its own, iso-density neither indicates nor excludes malignancy.

### Low Density

A low-density mass attenuates fewer X-rays than fibroglandular tissue but contains no fat. Low density on its own does not establish that a mass is benign.

### Fat-containing

A fat-containing mass includes visible fat. Such masses, for example oil cysts, lipomas, galactoceles and hamartomas, almost always represent benign lesions. When the radiologist judges the appearance characteristic, the finding is assessed as BI-RADS category 2 (benign) and needs no further work-up.
