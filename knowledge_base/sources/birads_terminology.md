# ACR BI-RADS 5th Edition — Mass Margin, Shape, and Density Terminology

## Mass Margin

Margin refers to the border of a mass as visualised on mammography. Margin characterisation is the single most predictive feature for distinguishing benign from malignant masses in the mammographic mammographic Masses dataset (Elter, 2007).

### Circumscribed Margin
A circumscribed margin is sharply defined, with an abrupt transition between the mass and the surrounding tissue. Circumscribed masses are almost always benign. The probability of malignancy for a completely circumscribed mass is approximately 2%. Examples include fibroadenomas, cysts, and lymph nodes.

### Microlobulated Margin
A microlobulated margin has short-cycle undulations along the border of the mass. This pattern carries an intermediate level of concern. Microlobulated margins may be seen in some fibroadenomas, but also in some low-grade carcinomas. The probability of malignancy is approximately 15–30%.

### Obscured Margin
An obscured margin is partially or completely hidden by overlying or adjacent tissue. Obscured margins cannot be fully assessed and therefore carry indeterminate risk. Follow-up imaging or additional views are typically recommended.

### Ill-defined Margin
An ill-defined (indistinct) margin has no clear demarcation between the mass and surrounding tissue. This pattern raises concern for malignancy. The probability of malignancy for an ill-defined mass is approximately 20–50% depending on other features.

### Spiculated Margin
A spiculated margin has lines radiating outward from the mass. Spiculation is the strongest positive predictor of malignancy in mammographic imaging. The probability of malignancy for a spiculated mass exceeds 80% in most published series. A spiculated mass should be considered malignant until proven otherwise.

## Mass Shape

Shape refers to the geometric form of the mass in the mammographic image.

### Round Shape
A round mass is spherical, with all diameters approximately equal. Round masses are most often benign (cysts, fibroadenomas). The probability of malignancy is low when shape is round and margin is circumscribed.

### Oval Shape
An oval mass is elliptical or egg-shaped, including those with two or three undulations. Oval shape is also generally reassuring, particularly when combined with a circumscribed margin.

### Lobular Shape
A lobular mass has an undulating contour with more than three lobulations. Lobular shape carries intermediate concern, particularly when combined with non-circumscribed margins.

### Irregular Shape
An irregular mass does not conform to any standard geometric shape. Irregular shape is associated with higher malignancy risk. When combined with spiculated margin, irregular shape is strongly associated with invasive carcinoma.

## Mass Density

Density describes the X-ray attenuation of the mass relative to an equivalent volume of fibroglandular tissue.

### High Density
A high-density mass attenuates more X-rays than an equal volume of fibroglandular tissue. High density is uncommon but raises concern when present.

### Iso-density
An iso-dense mass has the same attenuation as the surrounding fibroglandular tissue. Iso-density alone is not a reliable indicator of malignancy.

### Low Density
A low-density mass attenuates fewer X-rays than fibroglandular tissue. Low density is generally a reassuring feature.

### Fat-containing
A fat-containing mass includes macroscopic fat, which is almost always a sign of a benign process (lipoma, hamartoma, oil cyst, intramammary lymph node). Fat-containing masses require no further imaging workup.
